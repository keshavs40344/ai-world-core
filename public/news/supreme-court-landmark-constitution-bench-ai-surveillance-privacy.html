Supreme Court Constitution Bench Issues Historic Doctrine on AI Surveillance and Algorithmic Privacy

A unanimous 5-judge bench establishes strict constitutional limits on predictive policing, facial recognition deployment, and automated administrative scoring.

NEW DELHI — In a monumental jurisprudence judgment that will redefine digital civil liberties across the Global South, a five-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court of India has held that unregulated artificial intelligence surveillance, predictive biometric mapping, and automated administrative profiling violate the fundamental right to privacy enshrined under Article 21 of the Constitution.

Core Investigative Findings & Key Facts

  • ▪Unanimous 5-judge Constitution Bench ruling limits state algorithmic and biometric surveillance.
  • ▪Declares automated predictive crime scoring unconstitutional under Article 21.
  • ▪Mandates 72-hour automated purge rule for public CCTV facial recognition datasets.
  • ▪Enforces an inviolable 'Right to Human Explanation' for administrative AI decisions.

Constitutional Limits on Algorithmic Governance

The bench, in a comprehensive 248-page verdict, ruled that state agencies cannot subject citizens to persistent automated biometric surveillance or AI-based behavioral risk scoring without explicit legislative backing, demonstrated proportionality, and independent judicial oversight. The court explicitly outlawed 'predictive policing' algorithms that assign criminality likelihood scores based on historical socio-economic or residential cluster data.

Writing for the unanimous bench, the Chief Justice affirmed: 'The human being is not an algorithmic variable to be preemptively cataloged by predictive state neural networks. When an opaque algorithm dictates who is stopped, who is scrutinized, or who is denied welfare entitlements, the rule of law is replaced by the tyranny of arbitrary code.'

Mandatory Algorithmic Impact Assessments (AIA)

The apex court established a binding national protocol mandating that any government agency seeking to deploy computer vision, automated license plate recognition (ANPR), or facial recognition systems (FRT) in public spaces must conduct an Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) certified by an independent auditor. Furthermore, citizens subjected to automated automated administrative decisions retain an inviolable 'Right to Human Explanation' (RHE).

Key Directives of the Constitution Bench

  • 1. Ban on Predictive Crime Scoring: Retrospective statistical profiling declared unconstitutional.
  • 2. 72-Hour Data Purge: Public facial recognition footage in non-criminal areas must be automatically purged within 72 hours.
  • 3. Non-Derogable Human Review: No state entitlement or welfare benefit may be cancelled solely via automated AI recommendation.
  • 4. Independent Algorithmic Appellate Tribunal: High Courts directed to designate special appellate benches for automated civil grievances.

Civil Society & Tech Industry Response

Legal scholars and human rights advocates have hailed the decision as the most progressive global privacy landmark since the European Union's GDPR and AI Act. Domestic enterprise software and cloud organizations welcomed the clarity, noting that transparent algorithmic guardrails will spur institutional trust in domestic artificial intelligence innovations while shielding citizens from surveillance overreach.
