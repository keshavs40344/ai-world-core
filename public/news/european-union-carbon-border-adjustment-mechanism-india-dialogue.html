BRUSSELS — In crucial trade diplomacy ahead of the full financial enforcement of the European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), bilateral negotiators from India and the European Commission have advanced a structured framework for mutual recognition of carbon accounting standards, seeking to shield key domestic steel and aluminum producers from punitive import levies.

Navigating the Carbon Border Levy Challenge

CBAM, intended to prevent 'carbon leakage' by taxing carbon-intensive imports into the European single market, threatens to impose tariffs of 20% to 35% on Indian exports of primary steel, iron ore, and aluminium. In bilateral consultations held in Brussels, the Indian delegation presented verifiable data showcasing significant emissions reductions achieved via domestic energy efficiency programs and renewable power integration.

India's Chief Trade Negotiator stressed: 'Climate action must respect the principle of Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR). While Indian manufacturers are rapidly transitioning to green hydrogen and solar-powered electric arc furnaces, unilateral trade barriers that act as disguised protectionism must be replaced by mutual carbon verification pacts.'

Mutual Recognition of India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)

The focal point of negotiations is the formal recognition of India's nascent domestic Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) administered by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE). Under the proposed mechanism, carbon penalties paid by Indian mills within the domestic framework will be credited dollar-for-dollar against European border liabilities, preventing double taxation.

Bilateral Carbon Trade Framework

  • • Targeted Export Volume: Over $8.2 Billion of annual Indian steel and aluminium shipments to the EU.
  • • Carbon Credit Offset: Direct offsetting of domestic BEE-certified carbon credits against CBAM dues.
  • • Green Steel Transition: Indian blast-furnace mills accelerating transition to scrap-based electric furnaces.
  • • Joint Technical Verification: Bilateral scientific committee established to harmonize greenhouse gas MRV protocols.

Broader Free Trade Agreement (FTA) Synergy

Resolving the carbon border impasse is viewed as essential for unlocking the comprehensive India-EU Free Trade Agreement, with both parties targeting conclusion of the historic bilateral pact by mid-2027.

⚡ Strategic & Civil Ramifications

Safeguards domestic heavy industrial competitiveness while accelerating the deployment of green hydrogen and low-carbon industrial manufacturing.
