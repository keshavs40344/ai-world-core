Demand Moderation and Non-OPEC Supply Counterbalance

The stabilization in international petroleum pricing reflects a dynamic equilibrium between controlled output from the 22-nation producer cartel and surging production from non-OPEC suppliers, led by the United States, Brazil, and Guyana. Combined non-OPEC output is expanding at 1.4 million barrels per day (mb/d), comfortably accommodating global demand growth without triggering inventory deficits.

A Senior Energy Strategist at London Energy Capital commented: 'The crude market has successfully priced out extreme tail-risk geopolitical premiums. Physical refinery inventories in Rotterdam, Singapore, and Jamnagar are healthy, while steady tanker transit rates through the Suez and Bab-el-Mandeb corridors have stabilized crack spreads for diesel and jet fuel.'

Fiscal Dividends for Major Importing Economies

For large net crude-importing nations such as India—which imports over 87% of its crude oil requirements—crude stabilizing below $80 per barrel provides a decisive macroeconomic cushion. Every $10 reduction in the average Indian crude basket slashes the current account deficit (CAD) by roughly 0.5% of GDP and reduces domestic retail price inflation by approximately 30 basis points.
