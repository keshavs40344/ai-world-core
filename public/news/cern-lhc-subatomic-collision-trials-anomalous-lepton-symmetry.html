The Breakdown of Lepton Flavor Universality

According to the bedrock Standard Model, the fundamental weak interaction treats electrons, muons, and tau leptons with identical gauge coupling strength, differing only by their respective invariant masses—a principle known as Lepton Flavor Universality (LFU). However, in high-luminosity 13.6 TeV proton-proton collisions analyzed by the LHCb, CMS, and ATLAS consortia, neutral B-meson decays to electrons occurred with measurably higher frequency than decays to muons.

A Senior Research Fellow from the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) who co-leads the CMS analysis team stated: 'For five decades, the Standard Model has withstood every experimental challenge. But this 4.1 sigma anomaly in the b -> s l+ l- channel is refusing to disappear. If confirmed to 5 sigma discovery status, this constitutes indisputable proof of a fifth fundamental force of nature mediated by leptoquarks or a Z-prime gauge boson.'

Upgraded High-Luminosity Detector Sensors

The discovery was made possible by the recent installation of ultra-fast silicon pixel vertex detectors capable of resolving particle decay tracks within 30 picoseconds. These high-speed radiation-hardened detectors were developed in joint partnership between CERN, TIFR Mumbai, and the Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics in Kolkata.

LHC Experimental Physics Telemetry

  • • Collision Energy: 13.6 Teraelectronvolts (TeV) at 27-kilometer circumference ring
  • • Anomaly Significance: 4.1 Sigma (Probability of random fluctuation less than 1 in 25,000)
  • • Decay Channel: Rare flavor-changing neutral current (FCNC) b-quark to s-quark transitions
  • • Target Milestone: 5.0 Sigma definitive discovery threshold targeted across next run cycle

Implications for Dark Matter and Cosmological Asymmetry

Theoretical physicists note that the existence of novel leptoquark bosons could finally explain the longstanding mystery of baryon asymmetry—why our universe consists almost entirely of matter rather than equal parts matter and antimatter—and provide a direct bridge connecting the Standard Model to cold dark matter particles.
