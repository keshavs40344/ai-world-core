NEW DELHI — In a historic milestone for sovereign aerospace deterrence, the Defense Acquisition Council (DAC), chaired by the Union Defense Minister, has granted formal Acceptance of Necessity (AoN) for the joint co-development and domestic manufacturing of a high-thrust 110-kilonewton (kN) turbofan engine to power India's Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) fifth-generation stealth fighter.

100% Technology Transfer and Metallurgy Ownership

Unlike previous procurement programs involving license-assembly of foreign military hardware, the 110kN engine program mandates complete, unvarnished transfer of core technology (ToT). Crucially, this covers single-crystal turbine blade metallurgy, ceramic matrix thermal barrier coatings, and Full Authority Digital Engine Control (FADEC) source code algorithms, allowing India to modify and upgrade the engine independently without foreign veto.

The Chief of the Air Staff noted: 'Fifth-generation air dominance requires supercruise capability—the ability to sustain supersonic velocities without engaging afterburners. The sanctioned 110kN engine guarantees that the AMCA will fly faster, strike deeper, and maintain absolute air superiority across high-altitude Himalayan sectors and maritime strike zones.'

Stealth Features and Internal Weapons Bay

The AMCA Mk-2 will incorporate serpentine air intakes that shield turbine blades from enemy radar illumination, an internal weapons bay capable of carrying BrahMos-NG cruise missiles and Astra Mk-3 air-to-air interceptors, and radar-absorbent radar cross-section (RCS) reducing skin coatings developed by the Defence Metallurgical Research Laboratory (DMRL).

AMCA Mk-2 Key Technical Specifications

  • • Maximum Take-Off Weight: 25,000 kg (Twin-engine medium combat platform)
  • • Supercruise Speed: Mach 1.3+ dry thrust without afterburner engagement
  • • Radar Cross Section (RCS): Sub-0.001 sq meter (equivalent to a small bird)
  • • Avionics: Indigenous Uttam AESA Radar paired with Distributed Optical Sensor System

Industrial Aerospace Supply Chain Mobilization

The program will be executed through a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) led by the Gas Turbine Research Establishment (GTRE) in collaboration with private aerospace manufacturing majors in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Pune. Over 200 micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) specializing in precision machining and titanium forging have been integrated into the tier-1 supply network.

⚡ Strategic & Civil Ramifications

Eliminates the decades-old jet engine technology deficit and establishes India as an exporter of 5th-generation combat aerospace platforms.
