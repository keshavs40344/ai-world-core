Historic multilateral agreement mandates 20% real-time allocation of diagnostics, therapeutics, and vaccines to developing nations during public health emergencies.

GENEVA — In a decisive breakthrough after three years of intense multilateral diplomacy, the Intergovernmental Negotiating Body (INB) of the World Health Organization (WHO) has adopted a consensus draft for the landmark Global Pandemic Agreement, formally resolving contentious disputes over the Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing (PABS) system.

Equitable Access to Life-Saving Medical Countermeasures

The finalized agreement legally enshrines that whenever a novel pathogen with pandemic potential is identified and genetically sequenced, manufacturers utilizing that sequence data must allocate a minimum of 20% of their real-time production—10% as a donation and 10% at cost-price—to the WHO for immediate distribution to developing countries in Africa, Asia, and Latin America.

The head of the Indian delegation to Geneva remarked: 'The tragedy of vaccine nationalism witnessed during the COVID-19 pandemic cannot be repeated. India's steadfast position on equity and affordable technology transfer has been vindicated. Pathogen genetic data and life-saving remedies are public goods, not tools for geopolitical leverage.'

Decentralized Vaccine Manufacturing Hubs

Under Article 11, developed nations are obligated to facilitate voluntary technology transfers and license sharing to regional pharmaceutical manufacturing hubs in the Global South, including India's world-leading vaccine producers in Pune and Hyderabad, ensuring rapid regional surge capacity during disease outbreaks.

Key Provisions of the WHO Pandemic Accord

  • • PABS Quota: 20% mandatory global emergency allocation of vaccines and therapeutics.
  • • Sovereign Genetic Rights: Member states retain sovereign rights over biological samples.
  • • Rapid Technology Transfer: Voluntary licensing framework to accredited Global South manufacturers.
  • • One Health Surveillance: Integrated monitoring across wildlife, livestock, and human interfaces.

Unanimous Adoption Scheduled for World Health Assembly

The draft will be formally submitted for unanimous legal ratification at the extraordinary session of the World Health Assembly, establishing an enduring international architecture for global biosecurity.

⚡ Strategic & Civil Ramifications

Eliminates discriminatory vaccine nationalism and establishes permanent legal protections for affordable healthcare access in future international health emergencies.
