Non-flammable ceramic solid electrolyte completely eliminates fire risk while tripling the specific energy of conventional lithium-ion cells.

HYDERABAD — In what is hailed as the holy grail of clean electrochemical energy storage, scientists at the International Advanced Research Centre for Powder Metallurgy and New Materials (ARCI), in consortium with CSIR-CECRI, have successfully fabricated and demonstrated a commercial prototype solid-state Lithium-Sulfur (Li-S) battery cell achieving a record energy density of 612 Watt-hours per kilogram (Wh/kg).

Tripling Energy Density at Half the Raw Material Cost

Standard commercial lithium-ion batteries utilize heavy, expensive cobalt and nickel cathodes, limiting their gravimetric energy density to approximately 240 to 280 Wh/kg. The newly unveiled solid-state battery replaces toxic cobalt with abundant, low-cost sulfur, while utilizing a specialized non-flammable garnet-type ceramic solid electrolyte that completely eliminates thermal runaway and fire hazards.

The Lead Scientist at ARCI stated: 'At 600 Wh/kg, electric mobility breaks through the final range-anxiety barrier. A passenger electric car equipped with a battery pack of this density can comfortably travel over 1,200 kilometers on a single charge, while weighing less than half of an equivalent legacy battery.'

Unlocking Regional Electric Aviation and Heavy Long-Haul Trucking

Beyond passenger cars, the lightweight chemistry makes regional electric aircraft and heavy 40-tonne commercial freight trucks economically viable. Initial flight simulations on an experimental 9-seater electric commuter aircraft demonstrated sustained 90-minute cruising with 40% battery reserve margins.

Solid-State Li-S Performance Metrics

  • • Specific Gravimetric Energy Density: 612 Wh/kg (More than 2.3x standard LiFePO4).
  • • Thermal Safety: Stable up to 250°C with zero flammability or dendrite penetration risk.
  • • Cycle Life: 1,500 full charge-discharge cycles with 86% capacity retention.
  • • Cathode Material: Abundant industrial byproduct sulfur, eliminating cobalt-nickel dependency.

Pilot Gigafactory Integration in Gujarat

A collaborative public-private consortium has commenced construction of a 2 GWh pilot manufacturing facility at the Dholera Special Investment Region in Gujarat, with commercial production slated for late 2027.
