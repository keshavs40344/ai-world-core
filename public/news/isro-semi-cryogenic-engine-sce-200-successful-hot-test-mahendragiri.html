ISRO Successfully Hot-Tests Indigenous SCE-200 Semi-Cryogenic Engine at Mahendragiri: Paving Path for Heavy-Lift Next-Gen Launch Vehicles

The 2,000-kilonewton thrust engine fueled by Isrosene kerosene and liquid oxygen will replace the L110 core stage on LVM3, increasing GTO payload capacity to 6 tonnes.

MAHENDRAGIRI, TAMIL NADU — The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has achieved a watershed milestone in heavy-lift rocketry, successfully completing a full-duration hot-fire test of its indigenous 2,000-kilonewton thrust Semi-Cryogenic Engine (SCE-200) at the High-Altitude Test Facility inside the ISRO Propulsion Complex (IPRC) in Mahendragiri.

Transitioning to High-Density Isrosene Propellants

Unlike conventional cryogenic engines that consume both liquid hydrogen (-253°C) and liquid oxygen (-183°C), a semi-cryogenic engine pairs aerospace-grade refined kerosene (dubbed 'Isrosene') with liquid oxygen. Because refined kerosene is substantially denser than liquid hydrogen, it dramatically reduces the structural volume and dead weight of rocket fuel tanks while generating immense sea-level thrust.

The Chairman of ISRO announced from mission control: 'The successful firing of the SCE-200 marks the dawn of a new era in Indian space launch autonomy. It is one of the most powerful single-chamber rocket engines ever designed in Asia, delivering 200 tonnes of thrust. This engine will serve as the common booster core for our Next-Generation Launch Vehicle (NGLV) and human spaceflight lunar exploration.'

Boosting LVM3 Payload Capacity to Geosynchronous Orbit

In its immediate application, the SCE-200 semi-cryogenic stage (designated SC120) will replace the legacy Vikas-engine liquid core stage (L110) on India's heaviest operational rocket, the Launch Vehicle Mark 3 (LVM3). This engineering upgrade increases the LVM3's payload capacity to Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO) from 4,000 kg to over 6,000 kg, allowing India to launch heavy commercial communication satellites domestically without outsourcing to foreign providers.

SCE-200 Engine Technical Specifications

  • • Vacuum Thrust Rating: 2,000 Kilonewtons (204 Tonnes-force).
  • • Propellant Combination: Isrosene (Refined RP-1 Kerosene) and Liquid Oxygen (LOX).
  • • Engine Cycle: Staged Combustion Cycle with oxygen-rich preburner.
  • • Payload Uplift: Enhances LVM3 GTO payload from 4 tonnes to 6.2 tonnes.

Architectural Foundation for Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS)

Looking toward the 2035 deployment of the Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS) and crewed lunar landings by 2040, clustered configurations of SCE-200 engines will power the 100-tonne-to-LEO class Next-Generation Launch Vehicle (NGLV), named 'Soorya'.
