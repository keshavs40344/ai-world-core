NEW DELHI — In a monumental humanitarian victory for genomic medicine, the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) has granted full market authorization for India's first indigenously developed CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing therapy for Sickle Cell Disease and Beta-Thalassemia.

Curing a Genetic Affliction Prevalent in Tribal Populations

Sickle Cell Disease is a debilitating hereditary blood disorder affecting over 20 million citizens across central and western India, particularly within tribal communities. While Western biopharma giants commercialized gene-editing cures at exorbitant price points of $2.2 million (₹18 crore) per patient, the CSIR Institute of Genomics and Integrative Biology (CSIR-IGIB) engineered a proprietary high-fidelity Cas9 variant, delivering a permanent one-time curative treatment for less than ₹10 lakh.

The Union Minister of Science and Technology stated: 'Science has no meaning if its life-saving fruits remain out of reach for the poorest tribal families. By developing our own indigenous CRISPR enzymatic scissors, we have transformed an incurable lifelong suffering into an affordable, permanent cure.'

Ex-Vivo Stem Cell Editing and Clinical Trial Triumphs

The therapy extracts the patient's hematopoietic stem cells, utilizes the proprietary FnCas9 enzyme to reactivate the production of fetal hemoglobin (HbF), and reinfuses the edited cells following mild conditioning. In multi-center Phase-3 clinical trials across 240 patients, 96.8% of participants achieved total freedom from vaso-occlusive pain crises and blood transfusion dependence for over two years.

Indigenous CRISPR Clinical Trial Metrics

  • • Curative Efficacy: 96.8% of patients completely transfusion-free post-infusion.
  • • Cost Reduction: Slashed by over 95% compared to commercial US and European therapies.
  • • Precision: Zero off-target genetic cuts detected via whole-genome deep sequencing.
  • • Manufacturing: Distributed cellular therapy centers operational in Nagpur, Ranchi, and Bhopal.

Integration into the National Sickle Cell Elimination Mission

The approved therapy will be deployed through subsidized public health corridors under the National Sickle Cell Elimination Mission, with government health insurance (PM-JAY) covering treatment expenses for eligible underprivileged families.
