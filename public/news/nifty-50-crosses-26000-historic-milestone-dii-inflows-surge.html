Retail SIP Revolution Counterbalances Foreign Outflows

While global macro headwinds and elevated US Treasury yields prompted episodic foreign portfolio investor (FPI) profit-taking, domestic retail and institutional investors (DIIs) absorbed all selling pressure with unprecedented liquidity. Monthly Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) gross inflows breached ₹23,300 crore, creating an indomitable domestic liquidity buffer that has decoupled Indian equities from emerging market correlations.

The Managing Director of the National Stock Exchange stated: 'The Indian equity market is experiencing a profound democratization. Over 110 million unique demat accounts are now actively participating in the wealth creation cycle of our corporate economy. Reaching 26,000 on the Nifty is not an asset bubble; it is the mathematical reflection of corporate balance sheet health and consistent double-digit return on equity (ROE).'

Sectoral Catalysts: Capital Goods, Banks, and Power Grids

The rally was led by capital goods majors benefiting from multi-year government capital expenditure orders, alongside public sector banks reporting record low net non-performing asset (NPA) levels below 0.6%. Renewable energy utilities and power transmission conglomerates also posted outsized gains as national electricity peak demand crossed 250 gigawatts.

Institutional Outlook for Upcoming Quarters

Global investment banks, including Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, reiterated their overweight allocations on India, citing sustained macroeconomic stability, fiscal consolidation at 4.9% of GDP, and sovereign manufacturing tailwinds under the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) framework.
