Synthetic Aperture Radar & Automated AIS Correlation

The joint capability interconnects commercial low-Earth orbit (LEO) synthetic aperture radar satellites with national naval coastal surveillance radars. By continuously cross-referencing Automatic Identification System (AIS) broadcasts with thermal satellite scans, the system detects 'dark vessels'—ships that deliberately deactivate their transponders to conduct illicit offshore maritime incursions, sanctions evasion, or illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) maritime harvesting.

The Information Fusion Centre - Indian Ocean Region (IFC-IOR) located in Gurugram, India, serves as the primary operational nexus for the Western and Central Indian Ocean sectors, coordinating with liaison naval officers from over 24 partner nations.

Joint Freedom of Navigation Declaration

In a joint ministerial declaration released simultaneously in New Delhi, Washington, Tokyo, and Canberra, the four Quad powers reaffirmed their unwavering commitment to a free, open, inclusive, and rules-based maritime order founded upon the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS).

Deterrence Against Gray-Zone Intrusions

Strategic analysts point out that the operationalization of IPMDA effectively ends the era of covert maritime gray-zone coercion in the Indo-Pacific. Any naval militarized militia vessel attempting to intimidate sovereign coastal states or blockade commercial shipping arteries will be instantly exposed via transparent, public-domain satellite evidence.
